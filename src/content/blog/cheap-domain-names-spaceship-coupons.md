## Which extension should you pick

The .com is the safest default. It is the one people type from habit, so it suits a business site, a portfolio or a blog. At $3.80 with COM67 it costs little enough that I would only settle for something else if my name was already taken.

The .net is a good second choice when the .com is gone. It started out for networking companies and now gets used for all kinds of sites. At $4.98 with NET19 it is the priciest of the four here.

The .org fits communities, open source projects and nonprofits. At $3.44 with SPSR86 it also costs less than the .com.

The .info is the cheapest at $2.73. It works well for an information site, documentation or a side project you want to test without spending much. For a brand people need to remember, I would still lean towards the .com.

## Check these before you buy

Promo prices often apply to the first year only. Look at the renewal price before you pay, because that is what you will be charged from the second year.

Pick a name that is short and easy to say out loud. Hyphens and numbers make a domain harder to share with other people.

Buy only the domain you plan to use. A low price makes it tempting to register several names, and each one renews separately.
